Note: Data for January, February, March 1937 and January 1939 are 
missing at the AFRL library, and are available at Harvard's Cabot 
Science library.

Note that with 1937 most states have now stopped giving specific 
locations of auroral observations. The trend had started earlier, 
e.g., with North Dakota.

Data for Moorhead, Minnesota are sometimes reported in the North 
Dakota section pages.

During this period I have relied largely on GNIS for geographic 
coordinates of sites not previously listed. Where the site is not 
listed on GNIS, but where a Post Office address is given in 
Climatological Data, I have used coordinates of the Post Office. 
Where neither the site nor a Post Office address is given, but the 
county has been listed in Climatological Data (true almost always) 
I have averaged the latitudes and longitudes of the extremes of 
the coordinates for populated places given in GNIS.

		Utah: On April 16th, between the hours of 9:15 and 10:00 
p.m., an auroral display was noted by many observers, and in all 
except the extreme southern and southeastern counties of Utah. At 
a few stations a lesser display was observed on the night of the 
17th.

	Mr. R.B. Stone, Midvale, Utah, reported some careful 
observations of the auroral phenomena, substantially as follows:
"The display began as a reddish glow on the northeastern horizon. 
By 9:45 p.m., the time of greatest intensity, the aurora had 
assumed the shape of a great rainbow arch, extending from Mt. 
Olympus (on the east) to the northwestern horizon. The arch was 
bright red with white bands and showed a waving or furling motion. 
The display faded finally to red glowing patches of light which 
disappeared entirely about 10:00 p.m." - G.P.M.
