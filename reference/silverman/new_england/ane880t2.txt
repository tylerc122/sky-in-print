extending from nearly every point of the compass to the zenith and

N.West and again at the East. Frequently the beams from every 
direction would meet at the zenith, and form a corona. At the S.W. 
and S.E. there was the least display but the flashes frequently 
extended across these points. At 10 the aurora began to diminish, 
and at 10.30 after the moon had risen, was visible nowhere except 
at the North where beams and flashes continued. At 11 the moon was 
very bright and all signs of the aurora had disappeared.

1882    11   14  NEW LONDON          CONNECTICUT    
Auroral waves all night [Remarks]
From sunset 14th to daylight 15th (visible) - probably the same 
aurora noted on the 12th as there was evidence of a large quantity 
of electricity in the atmosphere during the 13th, while the 
horizon was observed. [Summary]

Auroral waves flashing all night across the whole horizon. No 
trace of an arch visible and the phenomenon had much the 
appearance of diffused sheet lightning. The sky was at no time 
clear of clouds and was at long intervals entirely obscured with 
light cirri strata which did not however entirely eclipse the 
auroral light. All electric wires were seriously affected during 
the day and night. [WB]

1882    11   17  NEW HAVEN           CONNECTICUT
*
At 5.36 a.m. nearly one hundred of the annunciators at the 
telephone office dropped simultaneously. This occured at irregular 
intervals throughout the day and night. Much trouble existed at 
the telegraph office, the wires working poorly, showing the 
existence of an electric storm. It appeared in the form of 
successive pulsations of alternately negative and positive 
electricity. The telephone wires were at times excessively charged 
and then again the currents were entirely neutralized. The falling 
snow and clouds prevented any auroral observation.

Moonlight night From 9 p.m. to Midnight a very brilliant aurora

defined, with streamers and flashes of light spreading in all 
directions and extending far south of the zenith, almost to the

whole heavens resembled a fiery dome, with a stationary nucleus at 
the zenith, supported by columns of different colors. The

Faint aurora from No 18th still visible at 3 a.m. pale streamers 
rising in the North and vanishing.

During the evening a most brilliant auroral display. From two 
points in the NE and NW horizon as centres, numerous pale yellow 
streamers shot forth forming an arched vault with the summit 
almost exactly in the zenith. A dark arch appeared about 20 
degrees above the horizon. Numerous black clouds seemed to form on 
the luminous background and to vanish as rapidly as they appeared. 
At 11.45 a very small strange black cloud exactly N.N.W. 12 
degrees above the horizon was most conspicuous. The aurora was of 
the same character all night; during the later stages of the 
display the yellowish streamers became crimson tinged at their 
extremities in a most beautiful manner. Spectroscopic observations 
showed the well-known yellow "aurora-line" in the yellow streamers 
as well as in the rose and crimson fringes. The lines of Nitrogen 
and Oxygen were not plainly discernible, only now and then. [WB]

Aurora began at 7.40 p.m. and continues. An irregular arch was

nothing was noticed and reports were sent as usual with the

a.m. The wire worked very well while battery was on. But very 
little disturbance on the wire is ever noticed here during auroral 
displays. [WB]

1882    12   20  MOUNT WASHINGTON    NEW HAMPSHIRE  
An aurora began at 7.10 p.m. ended at 9.35 p.m. Previous to the 
commencement of the aurora the sky was perfectly clear, and a calm 
- a dark segment extending about 6o above the Horizon was first 
observed followed by a diffuse light, extending 3 or 4 degrees 
above the segment. First appeared exactly in the north gradually 
extended to 25o west of north, followed by the same extension in 
the east of north. Following the diffuse light, which was of a 
white color tinged with blue, there appeared flashes of light 
beginning at the dark segment on the north, and extending to the 
west and east successively, and attaining an altitude of from 25 
to 75 degrees. The color at the segment was from a dark red to a 
blood red gradually extending to a straw color, at its highest 
altitude it was of a whitish color. The Beams were grouped 
together at times and then would separate, only a few being then 
visible, they gradually extended upwards and of a sudden 
disappear, after the disappearance of the aurora, a luminous cloud 
was observed, which covered a portion of the span on the north 
previously occupied by the aurora, this quickly died away leaving 
a clear sky.

A peculiar phenomena was observed immediately after the 
disappearance of the luminous clouds. It consisted of a dark band, 
extending from the north west to the south east, passing 
immediately through the zenith. It appeared to be about 2 degrees 
in width and lasted 25 minutes. It rose immediately from the 
horizon on the north west and by a quick motion extended itself to 
the south east. It was very distinct and perfectly stationary, and 
as suddenly disappeared. It was without color, appearing only as a 
dark band across the sky. During the aurora at no time did it 
reach the place occupied by this dark band, and after the 
disappearance of the dark band no other phenomena appeared. The 
moon shone brightly the whole night. [WB]

1883     7   29  BOSTON              MASSACHUSETTS   
Aurora began at 9.30 p.m. and continued all night.

A very bright aurora was observed tonight. At about 9:30 p.m. 
auroral lights were first observed in the northern heavens. They 
gradually became brighter until 10 oclock when the whole northern 
portions of the heavens from east to west and extending nearly to 
the zenith was covered with patches of bright light and waves of 
light constantly moving across them. This light was in constant 
commotion and there seemed to be patches of clouds moving towards 
the west but there were no clouds visible in any other portion of 
the sky. The most remarkable feature of this phenomenon was a  
bright band of yellowish light which seemed to be separated from 
the other part of the aurora. It was about 4o in width and 
extended from the western horizon to the zenith. This lasted from 
about 9:30 p.m. to 10 p.m. it remained nearly stationary and was 
very bright. At about midnight the aurora changed into the "Arch" 
form with the dark segment underneath. It continued all night but 
became fainter and quieter towards morning. [WB]

1883     7   30  BLOCK ISLAND        RHODE ISLAND             
Aurora 8.40 p.m. to 2.30 a.m. 31st

At 8.40 p.m. a luminous beam of auroral light shot up from the 
northern horizon followed in quick succession by others of a 
brilliant white color increasing in splendor with the darkness 
until 9.30 p.m. when the horizon from northwest to northeast was 
covered with narrow beams which reached to the altitude of the 
north star. At 10.35 p.m. these beams were merged into a brilliant 
white arch with its lower edge pink, 10 degrees above the horizon. 
Stars observed through the aurora were of a pinkish hue. The 
display was also accompanied by numerous shooting stars of unusual 
brilliancy. [WB]

1883     7   30  POINT JUDITH        RHODE ISLAND
The Aurora was visible during early a.m. until hidden by daylight. 
... At 8.50 p.m. Auroral beams of a pale straw color were observed 
shooting up about 40o. At 9.10 p.m. a low arch formed, and was 
visible until after midnight. At first it was of a pale yellow 
color, but it increased in brightness during the night. The arch 
was 10o in width, 8o above the horizon, resting on a dark cloud or 
dense haze, and extended from N.W. to N.E. At 9.30 p.m. parallel 
beams not connected with the arch below, formed between N. and 
N.W. and rapidly moving toward the W., disappeared, one at a time, 
at the same point. These beams were 10o in length, 35o above the 
horizon, and were visible 20 minutes. At 9.50 p.m. bright yellow 
beams, tinged with crimson, shot up from the arch about 15o. These 
beams had a slow motion toward the E. Beams observed after 10 p.m. 
apparently had no lateral motion. No beams were noticed after 
midnight.  [WB]
