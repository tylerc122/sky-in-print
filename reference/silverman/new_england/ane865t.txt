[Two clippings, February 21, sources not identified, relating to 
aurora of 2/20:] 
AURORA. As the Pleasant street clock struck 6 last evening, a 
brilliant arch about 6o or 7o in width spanned the heavens from 
East to West; involving in its luminosity the 4 stars in the head 
and neck of Leo: - passing about two degrees to the north of 
Castor; having Capella on the Southern edge; enclosing Perseus and 
a part of Andromeda in its folds, and thence coming more directly 
to the west. It was quite uniform in its width and brilliancy, but 
lasted only a few minutes. We hope it may have been seen at 
Philadelphia, and that Mr Marsh of that city will give us its 
height above the earth.     P.

The Auroral Arch of Monday eve, 20th inst., was not seen in 
Philadelphia, but very fortunately was seen by our townsman, Mr. 
A.S. Tenney, editor of the Telegraph, Brunswick, who, with a 
sidereal map in hand, located it among the stars. By means of his 
observations and those furnished by the Herald, Mr. Marsh has 
determined its height to have been 75 miles or more above the 
surface of the earth; which is about the same as that determined 
by him for the arch of Jan. 16th 1865.

auroral arch of Wednesday 2nd, is alluded to above. [see below] 
Friday evening the 4th a bank of cumulus, that had formed along 
the N, gradually swept seaward being at 10 p.m. in ENE. Diffused, 
with occasional zigzag lightning, had been playing along its edge 
and face, almost incessantly. From that hour, until its 
disappearance, auroral beams shot from its apparent margin, to the 
extreme W., in most beautiful changes and forms. During the entire 
night that followed, the electrical flashes were continual, and 
assuming about 2-1/2 a.m. of Saturday, an auroral center, at the 
zenith, from which radiated towards the horizon on every side, 
streams of light, making it a very interesting spectacle. Query. A 
neighbor who was necessarily up much of the night, said that the 
sound of the auroral flashes, could be distinctly heard. Was this 
so? Another peculiar form, in which the aurora borealis presented 
itself, on the evening of Tuesday 22nd, was in elongated clouds, 
stretching from either horizon at ENE. and WSW. and uniting at the 
zenith, forming a series of bands spanning the sky. Commenced at 
7.40 p.m.; continuing until 8.20 p.m., and then sweeping 
Southwardly, and breaking into fibrous fragments. At this time 
close unbroken nim. covered the sky, the auroral clouds apparently 
stretched out upon, or below it.

1866     2   21  GROTON              CONNECTICUT    
Aurora last night or rather this morning. I saw it first about 1/2 
past 12 midnight and for two or three hours. I think it must have 
lasted through the entire night as it was still visible at a 
little before daylight this morning. It was very bright and truly 
beautiful, seeming more like continual waves of golden lights 
rolling across the whole heavens. The coruscations were very rapid 
in their motion, and continual, also continually changing in 
colour, pale green red yellow sometimes bright wavy patches of 
light spring up from the horizon. About 1/2 past 12 there was a 
long black cumulus cloud lying along the northern horizon but the 
wind soon carried it away. I was up several times during the night 
the appearance of the aurora being about the same till morning. It 
was through the night almost as light as day, lighter than 
moonlight. This aurora was truly magnificent. I see by the papers 
that the telegraph lines were much affected.

1869     3    9  HOULTON             MAINE          
Observed the Aurora Borealis beginning at 7 p.m. and continuing 
till 10 p.m. It appeared in the form of beams shooting up from 
every quarter of the Northern hemisphere to a point midway in the 
heavens, where, upon meeting, the beams appeared to whirl around 
somewhat after the manner of clouds meeting in opposite 
directions. At about 8 p.m. the Southern Hemisphere of the heavens 
seemed to be lighted up by large sheets of the Aurora but somewhat 
undefined. The streams at the North were constantly changing both 
in intensity and form. Later in the evening a brilliant band of 
the aurora spread across the middle of the heavens from east to 
west about three degrees in width and about three fourths the 
distance in length from horizon to horizon. At one time two arches 
were formed at the north which developed into the most beautiful 
curtain form aurora, which at theier extremities swung around and 
joined each other. The color throughout was clear white save an 
occasional tinge of red. Such magnificent displays of the aurora 
are very rarely seen at this point.

Beams to the zenith, where they whirled, as if they met opposing 
currents. At 8 p.m. the southern horizon was lighted up, and later 
a band of light from east to west; color white, with occasional

Very brilliant aurora commenced at 9-1/2 o'clock p.m. in the form 
of an arch, then changed to the curtain form, then kept 
continually changing. At one time it showed the colors, pink and 
purple. At 9 o'clock it extended from the horizon to the zenith 
like a fan half open, the wider part being at the horizon. The 
light flashed up through it continually for a few minutes when it 
changed its form. It disappeared at 10 o'clock. Observed several 
shooting stars while watching the aurora.
