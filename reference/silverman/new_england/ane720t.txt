[Note: the following includes an extract from a book by Samuel 
Williams, originally at Harvard, then after difficulties with the 
College, resident at Rutland, Vermont. The location at which the 
aurora was observed is not given. It was either Cambridge or 
Rutland, though listed here as Cambridge.]

                         Aurora Borealis

Their Colour and Appearance. Sometimes red, but was generally of a 
pale light. - Appear like the Tails of Comets - Or like the flames 
of pale fire. - The Stars visible through them.

The Matter of the Aurora. Probably the same as the Tail of Comets, 
that of fire etc.

New or uncommon Phenomena of the Aurora in N. England. 
1. Their unusual Frequency. This has been very observable of late 
years etc.
2. Their appearance in the South
3. Their forming [?] a circular appearance or Corona near the 
zenith.
4. Probably attended with an hissing noise.

[one word illegible] on the Aurora of March 29, 1783.
Extent At 7-1/2 h it covered the whole heavens as far as Sirius, 
and reached from S.W. to N.E. -
Colour Vide [one word illegible] and Folsom's Gazette for Mar. 31, 
1783.
Appearance in the South. About 7-3/4 a small cloud of ye Aurora 
appeared in the S.W. The appearance seemed to be sudden and 
instantaneous without any gradual formation. This cloud in length 
was about 30o. In breadth it seemed to be about 10o. With a gentle 
and very regular motion it passed from the S.W. to the N.E. part 
of ye heavens where it suddenly vanished and disappeared. In its 
motion it went [?] at about 20o above the southern horizon. - In 
about 15' a cloud of Aurora about 1/2 the dimensions of the other 
appeared in the same part of the heavens in which the other 
disappeared, and moved in a contrary direction to the S.W. at the 
same altitude of 20o. In about 20' after this disappeared a third 
cloud appeared where the first began and moved to the N.E. its 
dimensions, motions, altitude and disappearance were in all 
respects similar to the first.
The appearance of a Corona. About 8o the streams from all parts of 
the horizon from S.W. to N.E. seemed to unite in forming a 
circular appearance. The diameter of this circle was about 12o 
[one word obscured] a small part of ye Aurora inclosed in and 
about the Center. [one word obscured] This circle or Corona 
streams seemed to proceed to the Southerly part of the heavens to 
the distance of 20 or 25o. This appearance continued about 45'. - 
The situation of it seems to [two words illegible] and was 
ascertained by the following circumstance. In order to find 
whether the dipping needle would be affected by the Aurora I [one 
word illegible] an excellent instrument of the kind and placed it 
on a post in my garden which was before [?] prepared for magnetic 
observations. The needle was placed very exactly [?] in the 
magnetic meridian. By repeated [one word illegible] I found the 
southern end of the Needle pointed very exactly [?] to the center 
of this Corona or circular appearance. The Dip was then from 69o 
to 69o-20. but I could not be certain whether it was affected by 
the Aurora or not as I had not made any obs. on the Dip for 
sometime before. It did not appear to have any uncommon vibrations 
[?] at that time. [BH]
