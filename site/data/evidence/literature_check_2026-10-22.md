# Literature check of current leads (2026-10-22)

**Method:** Internet Archive full-text search (`src/skyinprint/ia_fulltext.py`) over digitized journals, museum and
Smithsonian reports, and histories, with newspapers and post-1960 items excluded. A hit counts only when the place
name and a meteor word appear in the same matched passage. BHL and NYS Historic Newspapers sit behind bot checks
that a person has to clear, so they were not searched.

## Resolved by the literature
- **Northport, Me. (fragments 1881-84; fireball 1850): not a meteorite.** F. C. Robinson, *Am. J. Sci.* 3rd ser.
  35:212 (March 1888), "On the so-called Northport, Maine, meteorite":
  - Chemically and microscopically "nearly identical" with slag from the Revere Copper Works, probably ship's
    ballast.
  - He accepts that a fireball fell into the water there in 1850, but not that these pieces came from it.
  - Our newspaper reports of 1884-85 (a 20-lb mass held by Prof. Robinson) were the start of this same
    investigation.
- **Thomson, Ga. 1888: recognized meteorite, but an observed fall that the catalogue snapshot lists as "Found".**
  G. P. Merrill, *Smithsonian Misc. Coll.* 52:473 (1910):
  - B. F. Wilson's letter of Nov 26, 1888 says it "fell within thirty yards of where the writer was at work" on Oct
    15, 1888, while he picked cotton, and he dug it out with a spade.
  - USNM catalogue no. 395, 234 g, a chondrite.
  - Our contemporary newspaper report (Savannah Morning News, 1888-11-07) independently describes the fall.
  - This is a record correction (fell, not found), not a new meteorite. Next step: check the current Meteoritical
    Bulletin entry. The frozen NASA snapshot may differ from it.
- **Washington Co., Kan.:** every literature hit concerns **Farmington**, the recognized fall of 1890-06-25. Our two
  1888 reports (Marshall Democrat, Sept 1888; Hanover Democrat via WaKeeney, Oct 1888, Steele City, Neb.) predate it
  by two years. They are either a separate 1888 event, or mis-printed dates of some other fall. **Still open.**

- **Middleburg, Fla. 1888: limonite, not a meteorite.** G. F. Kunz, *Trans. N.Y. Acad. Sci.* 7:135 (Feb 1888), and
  *Science* 1888. Found on a second search for the Sub-Tropical Exposition (see `middleburg_trail.md`).
