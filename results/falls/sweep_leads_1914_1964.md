# Fall leads, sweep years 1914-1964 (read 2026-10-26)

## Ranking and reading
- **Reports:** 982 accepted fall reports, 1914-1964 (6,250 across all sweep years).
- **Read list:** 154 score 2 or more under the ranker as it stood before this pass. They collapse into 121 distinct
  stories once copies are grouped.
- **Claude read all 121:** 85 s of wall-clock time (`results/review_time.jsonl`).
- **Fewer reports than 1889-1913** (982 against 3,899), because far fewer later papers are digitized. This says
  nothing about how often meteorites fell.

## Recognized meteorites the read surfaced (validation)
- **Falls:** Colby (Wis., 1917), Richardton (N.D., 1918; the "Mott" fragments Prof. Treganza collected), Cumberland
  Falls (Ky., 1919), Pitts (Ga., 1921), Rose City (Mich., 1921), Johnstown (Colo., 1924; the "Elwell" fall), Miller
  (Ark., 1930; Julian Bailey's 37-lb stone, sold to a Rochester firm), Paragould (Ark., 1930), Archie (Mo., 1932),
  Athens (Ala., 1933), Pasamonte (N.M., 1933), Farmville (N.C., 1934), Dresden (Ont., 1939), Washougal (Wash., 1939),
  Sylacauga (Ala., 1954), plus N'Kandhla, Saffron Walden and others abroad.
- **Finds:** Navajo, Wallapai (the Hualapai reservation pair), Winona (the Elden Pueblo fragments), Aggie Creek (the
  "Annette Creek near Council" iron), Goose Lake, Drum Mountains, Eustis, and others.
- **Probably known, under another name:**
  - The Kenton County, Ky. iron (found 1889) is likely the "Piner" stone that George Cornelius sold to a Chicago
    museum. The 1916 story that it fell "eight years ago" is doubtful.
  - The Fruit Hill, Ky. stone (400 lb, bought by Prof. Ulrich of the USGS for the National Museum in 1903) is likely
    the Mount Vernon pallasite (found 1868, 13 km away).
- **Helt Township, Vermillion Co., Ind.** (seen to fall around 1921-24): it looked like the best new lead, but it is a
  described meteorite. Stuart H. Perry, "The Helt Township (Indiana) meteorite", *Smithsonian Miscellaneous
  Collections* 98(20), 1939; a 135 g slice is USNM 151172. It is missing from the NASA snapshot because the
  live Meteoritical Bulletin Database lists **"Helt Township" as a synonym for Canyon Diablo** (checked 2026-10-26). The
  witnessed-fall story attached to it was wrong: it is a fragment of the Arizona crater iron. **Resolved: not an
  Indiana fall.** **Lesson:** a "no catalogue match" in that snapshot is not enough. Each strong lead now also gets a
  literature search, and a same-year falls check out to 400 km, before it is called unrecorded.
- **Deep Gap / Riddles Fork, Watauga Co., N.C., July 9, 1929** (two sons of Joe Miller saw a 7-lb stone fall; a
  fragment went to Nininger in 1931): the same day as the recognized **Bald Mountain** fall (L4, 3.7 kg; ASU holds
  98 g, ASU no. 302). The catalogue puts Bald Mountain about 92 km away in Yancey County. A strewn field is rarely
  that long, so this is the Bald Mountain stone itself, or a second stone from the same fireball, or a location
  detail worth checking. **It is not an unrecorded fall.**
  The live database entry (checked 2026-10-26) gives only "Observed fall: Yes", 1929, 3.7 kg, and coordinates
  35°58'N 82°29'W. It names no county or finder and was never published in the Meteoritical Bulletin, so the 92 km
  question stays open.
- **Possible record note:** Eustis, Fla. is listed as "Found" (1918, 502 g). A 1917 paper calls John L. Algers's stone,
  sent to the National Museum, "the second meteoric stone that has fallen in Florida". That is weak evidence of an
  observed fall and needs the museum's record before it means anything.

## Third blind check of the ranker (`ranker_holdout3_draw.json`, drawn before reading)
- **Sample:** 100 fall reports scoring below 2, drawn by seed `ranker-holdout3-20261026` from 828 low scorers in
  1914-1964. Read in full.
- **Misses: 9 destinations in 100.** This is worse than the 4 in the second check. The later papers word things
  differently, and more line-end hyphens split words.

| H99 (Navajo) | "placed on ex- hibition" (line-end hyphen) |

- **At scale:** 9% of 828 is roughly 75 missed destinations among low scorers, against 154 flagged. So destination
  recall in these years is roughly two-thirds.
- **Fixes:**
  - Rejoin any line-end-hyphen split.
  - "sent to X for analysis/examination/assay".
  - "collected by Prof./Dr.".
  - "scientists/professors at X college", "X university scientists".
  - "purchaser ... museum".
- **Result:** 6 of the 9 are now caught. Left: H29 (OCR "n" for "to"), H25 (OCR), H92 (the penalty window crosses
  article boundaries).
- **Effect:** the patch adds 53 reports to the read list across all years (18 from 1914-1964, 35 earlier). All 53
  were read; the new earlier-year leads are listed above. **This sample is now used up.**
- **Leads in the sample itself:**
  - Yampa 1916, Springdale 1920, Roosevelt 1922, North Carver 1926, Toledo 1933.
  - Hardin, Mont. 1951 (an 8-lb stone found on melted snow, still warm).
