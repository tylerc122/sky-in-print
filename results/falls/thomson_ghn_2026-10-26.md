# Thomson, 15 October 1888: Georgia Historic Newspapers follow-up

Research date: 26 October 2026. New note; existing results left unchanged. No email or Gmail access.

## Main finding: an independent local sound report on the exact date

**The Lincolnton News (Lincolnton, Georgia), Friday, 19 October 1888, page 3, first column, “Local and Otherwise,” near the bottom.**

> —About 1 o’clock p. m. last Monday our citizens were startled by a heavy thundering report accompanied by a rushing noise. Speculations are rife as to what produced the report. Is it a recurrence of the explosions of ’83,’ and of previous years?

Transcribed from the page image, retaining the unusual punctuation around the abbreviated year. **The image reads ’83; OCR reads ’88.** The weekday and printed issue date resolve “last Monday” to **15 October 1888**. The item supplies a time, **approximately 1 p.m.**

**Assessment: strong research lead; medium evidence for an association with Thomson.** This is a contemporary local report referring to multiple citizens, with no mention of Wilson, Pitts, a recovered stone, or the Thomson Journal. It appears independent of the Wilson narrative. It is not a named witness deposition, does not describe a visible fireball, and does not identify the source of the sound. The date coincidence is excellent, but not proof of a common cause. The paper itself raises earlier unexplained explosions as an alternative context. No conclusion about a meteorite's trajectory or acoustic propagation is justified from this item alone.

The DLG's Lincolnton location is 33.79235, −82.47902; the NASA snapshot places Thomson at 33.46667, −82.48333. These points are approximately **36 km / 22.5 miles apart**, almost due north–south. That is a town-to-catalogue-point comparison, not a measurement to Wilson's precisely located cotton field. [DLG location metadata](https://dlg.usg.edu/record/dlg_ghn_sn90052031-1888-10-05-ed-1).

This is additional evidence relevant to the **recognized Thomson meteorite**, not a new unrecorded meteorite claim. The local snapshot identifies Thomson as L6, Found, 1888. Whether this sound report is sufficient to support reclassification remains for the catalogue editor and scientific assessment.

## Follow-up and checks on the sound report

- Visually inspected the full October 19 page and an enlarged crop; confirmed the date, local-column placement, 1 p.m., Monday, “thundering report,” “rushing noise,” and ’83.
- Searched all GHN titles over October 15–December 15 for “rushing noise.” Two pages returned: this item and an unrelated historical comet discussion in Savannah on October 21. No corroborating reprint in those results.
- Searched the Lincolnton title from October 19, 1888 through January 31, 1889 for `explosion report rushing thunder meteor earthquake noise` (37 pages). Reviewed relevant sound/explosion contexts; **no explanation or retraction found**.
- Separately retrieved all eight available Lincolnton page-3 local pages from October 26 through December 14 and checked report/sound/noise/shock/Monday contexts. No follow-up explaining the October 15 noise found.
- Searched statewide Lincolnton + explosion/thunder/report/noise through January 31 (37 pages), without finding another matching account.
- Exploratory retrospective search in the Lincolnton title, 1883–October 18, 1888, returned 64 pages for explosion/earthquake/rumbling. Local-page results include the 1886 earthquake and aftershock discussion. This did **not** establish what the October 19 writer meant by the explosions of ’83. The historical comparison remains unresolved, not a debunk.
- General web searches did not identify a scholarly connection between this Lincolnton item and Thomson.

These are OCR-dependent negative searches, not exhaustive page-by-page proof of absence. The phrase “no debunk found” must not be shortened to “not debunked.”

## Original Thomson paper and the disputed continuation

GHN does have **McDuffie weekly journal**, LCCN sn85034185, but only five digitized 1888 issues were returned: **February 22; November 2, 9, 16, 23**. Within the requested window that is **four issues / sixteen pages**. There are no digitized October 1888 issues in this result, and no November 30 or December issues.

I searched all sixteen pages' OCR for Wilson, Pitts, meteor/aerolite, cotton-field, whizzing, explosion/rumbling/noise and related wording, and visually checked the November 2 editorial and local pages (2 and 3). I did not locate the original Wilson/Pitts item. I did not visually read every surviving page, so an OCR miss remains possible. Missing October issues, particularly October 19 and 26, are worthwhile microfilm targets, not proven publication dates for the item.

**Editorial correction:** the November 2 page-2 masthead explicitly reads **“J. E. WHITE, Editor & Proprietor.”** Pitts should not be described as the editor on this evidence. “W. M. Pitts in Thomson Journal” in the reprint is an attribution to Pitts, not proof of editorship.

[November 2 editorial page](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn85034185/1888-11-02/ed-1/seq-2/).

A second reprint was located in **The Griffin Daily News, November 9, 1888, page 3**:

> W. M. Pitts in Thomson [Journal]: Ben Wilson brought to my office […] a metoric stone to be analyzed […] about a pound in weight […] encrusted with a dark, condensed, sooty matter.

This abbreviated quotation is based on OCR, not a checked transcription; the supplied OCR omits or damages words. It reproduces the specimen description and leads into the Cumming Clarion church item. It does **not** resolve the detached “He was in the cotton field” paragraph. It is another derivative report, not another fall witness.

[Griffin page](https://gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/lccn/sn89053182/1888-11-09/ed-1/seq-3/).

The known Savannah November 7 page remains the source for the displaced whizzing paragraph. Its connection to Wilson is plausible but is still not demonstrated by an original Thomson clipping here. [Verified existing LOC source](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86063034/1888-11-07/ed-1/?sp=6).

## Search scope and reproducibility

Used the GHN Advanced Search form with October 15–December 15, 1888, then the site's public JSON search results for OCR, issue enumeration and supplied IIIF image links. The human-facing page viewer presented an access verification screen; the public search and image services supplied the material used here.

Principal searches:

- `meteor meteoric aerolite rumbling` (OR): 100 pages. Reviewed keyword contexts; no independent Thomson-area event identified in these results.
- Phrase `ball of fire`: 5 pages. Included unrelated animal-eye metaphors, a recurring ghost-light story, general astronomy, and a later Minden, Louisiana fireball.
- `Thomson McDuffie` (OR) + Wilson: 46 pages; yielded Griffin's derivative reprint.
- `whizzing minnie` (OR) + cotton: 161 pages; keyword screening recovered the known Savannah item, not a properly attached original continuation.
- Phrase `Ben Wilson`: 2 pages, Savannah and Griffin.
- Thomson + `explosion report noise roar cannon` (OR): 70 pages, mostly unrelated page-level matches and advertisements.
- **Statewide `explosion aerolith fireball`, October 15–25: 41 pages. This is the query that found the Lincolnton report.** It had no meteor keyword and fell outside the initially requested city list.

The common word `report` was searched with locality restrictions; this was not a statewide examination of every occurrence of that word over two months. The retrospective and follow-up searches extended beyond the requested dates only to test the promising sound report.
