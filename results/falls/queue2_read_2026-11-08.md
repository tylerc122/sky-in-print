## Rawlett 1878 and the Allen meteorite
- **Allen's history** (Monnig, "A Meteorite From Allen, Collin County, Texas", 1946 typescript, and an undated report;
  TCU Monnig Papers MS 124, Box 05; read in TCU's digital repository): Willie Chandler picked up an odd stone on his
  160-acre farm in the J. Baccus Survey, sometime after WWI, maybe around 1923, and tossed it aside. He found it again in
  the creek in the winter of 1937-8, and it was identified at North Texas State Teachers College in 1938. The farm is
  on a small tributary of **West Rowlett Creek**, 4¾ mi NW of Allen; Monnig gives 33°07' N, 96°45' W. 1,409 g, a nearly
  complete individual. No fall story is recorded; Chandler's neighbors included the Rasors.
- **Why it's interesting:** the 1878 witness is "Mr. Rawlett"; Rowlett Creek (named for the pioneer Rowlett family) runs
  through exactly this country from Collin County south toward Dallas County. Monnig noted the stone's gray, barely rusted
  crust made it look like a recent fall, but the travertine crust implied a long time in the soil. A fall decades
  before the find would fit both.
- **Against it:** the find is about 24 mi N of downtown Dallas, not 15; Rawlett's stone was "of no mean size" while Allen
  is 3 lb; the find came ~45 years later on a farm whose 1878 owner is unknown; Rowlett was a common local surname.
- **Next:**
  1. Who was "Mr. Rawlett" in 1878: 1880 census, Collin and Dallas counties; Rowlett land along Rowlett Creek.
  2. Who owned the J. Baccus Survey tract in 1878 (Collin County deed records).
  3. The rest of the *Dallas Daily Herald*, Mar-Apr 1878, for a follow-up (only 2 copies are in our corpus).
  4. The Monnig Museum (TCU), which holds the Allen main mass and Monnig's trip notes.

### Digging, 2026-11-08
- **Scan checked** (*Dallas Daily Herald* 1878-03-09 p. 4, "The Fall of a Meteor"): it really says "a Mr. Rawlett" and
  "some fifteen miles north of this city". "Thursday evening" = **1878-03-07, about 4 pm**. Evening Star reprint 03-23.
- **No other report in the keyword corpus**, 1877-79. A full-text rescan of 1878-79 for any Rawlett/Rowlett mention or a
  meteor near Dallas-area names is in `data/work/rescans/1878_rawlett.jsonl` (see below once it finishes).
- **The Rowlett name is a place here, not a family.** Dr. Daniel Rowlett patented the land and the creek took his name,
  but he lived in Fannin Co. and died in 1848 (collincountyhistory.com, rowlett.html and rowlette.html). The **Rowlett
  community** sat at present SH 121 and Custer Rd, where Plano, Allen and Frisco meet, on the headwaters of Rowlett
  Creek; the Baccus brothers (1845) "settled west of Rowlett", and Chandler's farm is in the J. Baccus Survey. So the
  Allen find spot is right next to the old Rowlett community.
- **No Rawlett/Rowlett household found** north of Dallas in the 1870s in what's public (web searches; census needs a
  FamilySearch login).
- **Distance** from the Dallas County courthouse, straight line: Allen find 23.7 mi, Rowlett community 22.4 mi, Plano
  18.0, Richardson 12.7. "Fifteen miles" by 1878 roads would be closer to Richardson or south Plano than to Allen.
- **Reading:** the place-name match is real, but it may be a coincidence. One hypothesis worth keeping: the Herald
  garbled "a man from Rowlett" into "a Mr. Rawlett". That would fit the find spot, but the "15 miles" doesn't, and
  nothing confirms it. Allen stays a find unless one of the next steps turns something up.
- **Full-text rescan, 1878-79** (`rescan_names.py --only rawlett`, 50 hits in 1879, more in 1878; every hit read):
  - **No follow-up anywhere:** no later mention of Rawlett's stone, its size, or anyone examining it. Beyond the Herald
    and Evening Star, only reprints: *Connecticut Western News* 03-28, *Vermont Phoenix* 03-29, and a joke in the
    *Cincinnati Daily Star* 04-02 ("Meteoric stones ... are falling in that locality").
  - **The Herald itself spells the creek "Rawlett":** *Dallas Daily Herald* 1878-03-03, a road report on "bridges across
    White Rock, Duck, Rawlett and Muddy creeks". The same paper spells it "Rowletts creek" in a 03-23 legal notice. So in
    this paper's own usage Rawlett = Rowlett. That makes "Mr. Rawlett" either a Rowlett or a man known by the creek he
    lived on.
  - No Rawlett/Rowlett person anywhere in Texas papers of 1878-79 except T. M. Rowlett of Rockdale (died Feb 1878,
    Milam Co., far away).
- **Updated reading:** the fall most likely happened somewhere along Rowlett Creek, which runs ~30 mi from the Allen/Frisco
  headwaters through Plano and east of Richardson to Garland. "Fifteen miles north" fits the Plano stretch. Allen was
  found at the headwaters, maybe 5-10 mi upstream of that, and creeks don't carry a 3 lb stone uphill. So: same creek,
  probably not the same spot. The link stays possible, not shown.
- **Census (FamilySearch, 2026-11-08), surname Rowlett with variants, all Texas:**
  - **1870:** 30 hits, none in Dallas, Collin, Denton or Rockwall counties (Fannin, Nacogdoches, Grimes, Jackson, Hunt,
    Panola, Bastrop).
  - **1880:** 76 hits, nobody in Collin. **The only one in Dallas County is Luther Rowlet**, 28, single, farmer, born
    Tennessee, a boarder in the household of Martin Joyce, Precinct 4, ED 64, sheet 249D
    (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MFNS-L3H). He'd have been 26 in March 1878, and he's the only real
    "Mr. Rawlett" candidate north of Dallas. A boarder doesn't usually have "his place", but he could have been
    farming land nearby in 1878.
  - Anderson Rawlett (b. 1833 Ky.) is at Bonham, Fannin Co., ~60 mi NE: wrong place for "15 miles north of Dallas".
- **Next, needs a person:**
  1. Where was Dallas County Precinct 4 / ED 64 in 1880 (the enumeration district description, NARA T9 roll 1299)?
     If it's the north end of the county around Richardson, Luther Rowlet is very likely our man. Also the 1878 Dallas
     County tax roll for him.
  2. Monnig Museum (TCU): has anyone connected Allen to an 1878 fall; what is its weathering grade (W0-W6), which would
     say whether ~45 years in the soil fits.
  3. Collin County Genealogical Society (*Collin Chronicles*): a Rawlett/Rowlett in the 1870s near Plano/Rowlett.
