# Thomson, Ga.: refining the fall coordinates (2026-10-27)

This note evaluates the earlier catalogue position, 33° 28' N, 82° 29' W, using published historical records.

## What the sources say

- Merrill, *Smithsonian Misc. Coll.* 52:473 (1910), quoting B. F. Wilson's letter of Nov 26, 1888 to I. C. Plant:
  "It was on the place of Mrs. M. A. Wilson, in McDuffie county, four miles south of Thomson. It fell within thirty
  yards of where the writer was at work."
  https://archive.org/details/smithsonianmisce521910smit/page/473/mode/1up
- Merrill also quotes the Thomson postmaster: Wilson was picking cotton, the stone was "some thirty steps away" and
  "buried some six or eight inches in the earth".
- Same article: the stone weighed 234 g as received (USNM 395).
- The Savannah Morning News (1888-11-07, p. 6) and the Thomson Journal item it reprints say only "near Thomson".

## Estimate

- The catalogue point is effectively the town: about 0.3 km from central Thomson (33.4708, -82.5047), slightly
  east of it.
- Four statute miles (6.4 km) due south of central Thomson: **33.413, -82.505 = 33° 24.8' N, 82° 30.3' W**,
  about 6.3 km from the current catalogue point.
- Uncertainty: "four miles" is probably a rounded road distance and "south" a rough bearing. Call it ±2 km. To two
  arc-minutes: **33° 25' N, 82° 30' W**.
- This rests on the finder's single statement. No independent location is known.

## What would tighten it (not done)

- Find Mrs. M. A. Wilson in the 1880 census for McDuffie County (FamilySearch, free account). Her militia district
  (GMD) would confirm the south side of Thomson and narrow the area.
- McDuffie County tax digests or deeds for M. A. Wilson land, plotted on an old county map.
- Find a Grave has a "Wilson Family Cemetery", said in Pearl Baker's *'Neath Georgia Sod* to be "south of Thomson on
  the old J. G. Stovall place". The Find a Grave pin (33.49617, -82.53050) is northwest of town, so it is unreliable,
  and the family is not shown to be hers. It is a lead only.

## Who Wilson was (FamilySearch, checked by hand 2026-10-27)

- 1880 census: Benj. F. Wilson, 26, farming, white, head of a household of siblings. Thomson district, ED 75, McDuffie
  County (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M8G6-7XY).
- Family Tree M4F3-HQV: Benjamin Franklin Wilson, 25 Oct 1854 to 14 Mar 1907. That fits Merrill's 1910 note that
  "Mr. Wilson is now dead". Parents Elias Wilson (1809-1879) and Harriet Adeline Wall (1815-1879). Wife Miranda R.
  Armstrong (1861-1902).
- So "Mrs. M. A. Wilson" is not his mother.
- **Most likely: Martha Ann (Story) Wilson, 1843-1928 (K8M9-QT6).** She was the widow of Ben's brother James Marion
  Wilson (1844-1882, M4F3-H3M), so by 1888 she was a widowed "Mrs. M. A. Wilson" with a farm.
  - 1880 census: "Marion Wilson" household, Thomson district, ED 75, sheet 406C, household id 12670639
    (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M8G6-MXH).
  - Ben's household is sheet 408D, id 12670675, about 36 households further along the same enumerator's route. They
    were neighbours in the same part of the district.
- Ben's wife, Miranda R. Armstrong (married 22 Jan 1884), is less likely. Her 11 sources give "R."; Find a Grave's
  "Miranda Armstrong Wilson" is its maiden-name display, not a middle initial.
- Burial: Sweetwater Baptist Church Cemetery, McDuffie County (Find a Grave index,
  https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QVKZ-RL56).
  - The church is at 1606 Wrens Hwy (GA 17). The Census geocoder puts that at 33.434, -82.489, an interpolated
    address about 4.3 km SSE of central Thomson.
  - Sweetwater Creek and the Sweetwater Inn (33° 24' 58" N, 82° 27' 19" W) are a little further southeast.
  - A burial place is not a farm, but it puts the family in the Sweetwater community, south-southeast of town. That
    fits "four miles south".

## Revised estimate

- Four miles along the Wrens road (GA 17, bearing about 160 degrees) from central Thomson gives about 33.416, -82.481.
- Four miles due south gives 33.413, -82.505.
- Either way: **about 33° 25' N, 82° 29-30' W, roughly ±2 km**.
- The catalogue longitude was already about right; the latitude is about 3' (5-6 km) too far north.

## County records (FamilySearch full-text search of McDuffie County books, read 2026-10-27)

**Who "Mrs. M. A. Wilson" was: settled.**
- McDuffie Will Books 1872-1927: "J. M. Wilson Last Will", April 1882. Item 3 gives "my beloved wife Martha A. Wilson"
  his "entire property both real and personal". Item 4 names her Executrix.
- Court minutes, 1882: "Martha A. Wilson duly qualified as Executor of ... James M. Wilson".

**Tract A: not the fall site.**
- 200 acres in the 134th District G.M., on Little Germany Creek, north of Thomson.
- Bought 17 May 1883 from Henry Williams by Martha as executrix (Deed Books 1883-1886).
- Sold by Martha A. Wilson to Chas. W. Fling on 15 Nov 1887 for $600. Bounded N by B. F. Reese, E by H. C. Fulton,
  S by Thomas E. Watson, W by Henry Scott (Deed Books 1886-1889, image 297, p. 518).
- She sold it eleven months before the fall, and it lies north of town.

**Tract B: the Elias Wilson homestead. This is almost certainly "the place of Mrs. M. A. Wilson".**
- Deed Books 1880-1883, image 98, pp. 104-105: E. R. Wilson, administrator de bonis non of Elias Wilson Jr, to
  Henry O. Williams.
  - Sold at public outcry on 2 Nov 1880 for $2,127.24; deed dated 6 Dec 1880.
  - "two hundred and Eighty Nine (289) acres more or less, and bounded North by land of A. B. Thrasher, East by lands
    of Thomas Armstrong and John E. Langford, South by lands of Adam Simons and Noel Humphreys, and West by lands of
    J. C May being the same tract wherein the Said Elias Wilson Jr resided at the time of his death".
  - Elias Wilson (1809-1879) is Ben's father.
- Court writs 1879-1897 show a note from J. M. Wilson to H. O. Williams dated 6 Dec 1880, the same day, later paid by
  "M A Wilson Extrx J. M. Wilson". So James apparently bought the homestead back from Williams on credit, and it
  passed to Martha under the will.
- Deed Books 1895-1900, image 275, p. 478: on 29 Oct 1898, H. McCorkle, administrator of H. O. Williams, quitclaims
  the same 289 acres (same neighbours) to Martha A. Wilson. This clears the title.
- So in Oct 1888 the place of Mrs. M. A. Wilson was Ben's late father's homestead. Ben was working his widowed
  sister-in-law's farm, the farm he grew up on.
- Thos. Armstrong, the east neighbour, is Ben's father-in-law (Miranda's father). He, Elias, Ben and Miranda are all
  buried at Sweetwater Baptist Church, south of Thomson. That fits Wilson's "4 miles south".

**Coordinates.** Unchanged at about 33° 25' N, 82° 29-30' W, roughly ±2 km.
- 289 acres is about 1.2 sq km, so the farm itself is only about 1 km across. The remaining uncertainty is where the
  homestead sat, not how big it was.

**Not settled.**
- Ben's own 1880 census page shows none of the deed's neighbour surnames.
- Simons Road (OSM, 33.472, -82.561, west of town) is a family-name road but not tied to this Adam Simons.

**Next steps to pin it down.**
- McDuffie "Plat books, 1880-1916" are on FamilySearch. Look for a plat of the Elias Wilson / M. A. Wilson place or
  of a neighbour (Thrasher, Armstrong, Langford, Simons, Humphrey, May).
- Place Thomas Armstrong's land, which bounds the homestead on the east. It may be described with a creek or road in
  his own deeds or estate (he died 1895).
- Look for an 1880s Georgia militia-district map showing GMD 134's southern part.

## Placing the homestead from the neighbour chain (2026-10-27, later)

There is no plat of the Wilson place in the plat books: full-text search of the poor OCR found nothing. The deeds tie
the neighbourhood together instead.

**The neighbours connect.**
- Hardaway to Adam Simons bond for title, 1874 (Deed Books 1875-1880, image 121): 172 acres "adjoining land of Joel
  Humphrey, Elias Wilson J. E. Lang[ford] and Mrs. Rowland". This matches the homestead's south side (Simons,
  Humphrey).

**The anchor.**
- New England Mortgage Security Co. to Thos. E. Watson, 13 Dec 1889 (Deed Books 1888-1895, image 187, pp. 286-287).
  The land was first deeded 18 Dec 1882 and is recorded in Book C, pp. 558-559.
- "The Seven Hundred (700) acres of Land Situate on Sweet water Creek and bounded on the North by the lands of Mrs.
  Watson, James B. Neal and W. C. Dillon, on the East by lands of Albert Holliman, Georgia Railroad and W. H. Johnson;
  on the South by the lands of J E Langford and Thomas M Armstrong - and on the west by the lands of A. B. Thrasher,
  and Mrs. Burnside".
- So Watson's Sweetwater Creek tract lies north of Langford and Armstrong and east of Thrasher. The Wilson homestead
  lies south of Thrasher and west of Langford and Armstrong. The homestead therefore sits just southwest of Watson's
  700 acres, across the Langford/Armstrong corner.

**Where Watson's tract is.**
- Sweetwater Creek runs south from near the Georgia Railroad: USGS 02197530 "nr Bonesville" at 33.438, -82.451;
  USGS 02197540 at 33.416, -82.453; mouth about 33.367, -82.467.
- The Georgia Railroad runs ESE from Thomson (33.466, -82.503) through Boneville (33.433, -82.439) to Dearing
  (33.413, -82.385).
- A tract "on Sweet water Creek" bounded east by the railroad sits on the upper creek just southwest of the line,
  about 33.43-33.45 N, 82.45-82.47 W.

**Where that puts the homestead.**
- Southwest of Watson's tract: roughly 33.42-33.43 N, 82.47-82.49 W.
- That is the Sweetwater church neighbourhood (church 33.435, -82.486). Elias, Ben, Miranda and Thos. Armstrong are
  all buried there.
- It is 2.5-3.5 miles SSE of central Thomson in a straight line, which is plausibly "4 miles" by road via the Wrens
  road.

**Revised estimate: about 33.425 N, 82.48 W (33° 25.5' N, 82° 29' W), roughly ±1.5 km.**
- This is about 1.3 km from the earlier "4 miles due south" point and agrees with it. It now rests on the deed chain
  (Elias Wilson homestead, then Martha A. Wilson) and not only on Wilson's distance.
- Caveats:
  - The chain links each tract only by named neighbours, and Watson's 700 acres (2.8 sq km) is itself only roughly
    placed.
  - "Wilson" by itself is common locally, so only the named tract links are used.
  - A modern parcel or plat tie (Thrasher, Langford or Armstrong land in the 1890s plat books, or a 1900s county map)
    would be needed to get below about 1 km.
