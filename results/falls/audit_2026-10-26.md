# Audit of the fall leads, 2026-10-26

A second pass over the whole repo: lead write-ups, outreach log, ranker, sweep triage (245,249 articles, 6,250 accepted
fall reports) and the full-sweep fireball events. Nothing here has been checked outside the corpus unless it says so.

## 1. Fix before it lands

- **Helt Township is not a missed meteorite. It is Canyon Diablo.** Perry's 1939 specimen was later shown to be a
  Canyon Diablo fragment wrongly attributed to an Indiana fall (TCU Library record of Perry 1939:
  https://repository.tcu.edu/handle/116099117/61655). So the NASA snapshot is right to leave it out. The uncommitted edit
  to `sweep_leads_1914_1964.md` draws the opposite lesson ("simply missing from the snapshot"). The real lesson: an old
  published description isn't the last word either. Check the current status, not just the first paper.
- **Carrington 1910 email (scheduled today):**
  - The "Jan 10" date comes from the Beck Clothing Co. ad that leased the stone (Fargo Forum 1910-02-09). The first
    dispatch is dated Jan 15 and says the stone "kept hot" for more than 24 hours, which puts the fall around Jan 13-14
    (https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85042224/1910-01-15/ed-1/?sp=1).
  - Aug 1910 names the owner as Mr. Putnam, on show at Thornhill's Darlington store, about 350 lb. The draft says the
    Mowery farm, the Beck Clothing Co. and 500 lb.
  - Contemporaries were skeptical. Newark Evening Star 1910-01-17 mentions "denials"; Bismarck Tribune 1910-01-19 says
    "Darlington has a press agent".
  - A man was arrested in Feb 1910 for selling "chips from the meteor" made of melted beer bottles
    (https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85042224/1910-02-08/ed-1/?sp=2). Worth telling the historical society: any
    "Carrington meteorite" chip in a local collection may be glass.
  - Grade: white heat, hot for 24 h, "65 inches" and "many tons" are folklore markers. Medium-strong → **medium-weak**.

## 3. Medium leads, also never read

- **Coffeeville, Miss., 1894-08-16:** a fireball burst over the town; "volcanic rock"-looking fragments were picked up in the streets. 14 articles. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85042461/1894-08-17/ed-1/?sp=2
- **Victoria, Tex., 1891-05-07:** a daylight detonation along 100 miles of railway; a train crew saw a head-sized piece land 100 yd away. 38 articles. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86071158/1891-05-08/ed-1/?sp=3
- **Sycamore, Ohio, 1899-09-14:** a syndicated Tiffin dispatch: 500 lb, burst. 25 copies, no follow-up read yet. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82015679/1899-09-15/ed-1/?sp=5
- **Minerva, Ky., 1916-01-12:** J. D. Willett saw the fall and "thinks he has several pieces". It matches an 8-city Ohio Valley fireball that morning. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85038022/1916-01-15/ed-1/?sp=2
- **Hagerstown, Md., July 1913:** a 7-lb stone through the slate roof of the National Biscuit Co. building, "resembles crude cast-iron", to be analysed. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85025007/1913-07-26/ed-1/?sp=4
- **Kingston (Meeker Co.), Minn., ~1887:** a 200-lb stone ploughed up in 1891 and left with the Frank Daggett G.A.R. Post in Litchfield. The post hall survives as a county museum. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn91059394/1892-04-27/ed-1/?sp=1
- **Le Sueur, Minn., "fell fall 1868":** a 1,000-lb stone the city put in Stewart Park in 1896. Probably an erratic, but it's a cheap check. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83016758/1896-09-17/ed-1/?sp=3
- **Marshall Co., Kan.:** an 8-lb "meteor that fell in Marshall county" was in the KSHS museum in 1921. A fragment was on show at the Marysville (Marshall Co. seat) courthouse in 1888. These may be the same object; the Washington Co. 1888 lead and the 1921 item were never linked. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82016014/1921-12-10/ed-1/?sp=13
- **Finds in collections:** Cornish, N.H., 14.5 lb (Fairbanks Museum, St. Johnsbury, 1900); Helena, Mont., 90 + 70 lb nickel-iron (1893 World's Fair); Little Rockies, Mont., 150-lb iron (1899, "to the Smithsonian"); Eureka Co., Nev., 100 lb (Univ. of Nevada, 1905); Millington, Md. - **Lower:** Belair Market, Baltimore (1907); Stafford Courthouse, Va. (1910); Oskaloosa (1914); Griswold (1909); Rockport, Me. (1892); New Sweden, Minn. (1891); Oyster Bay, Sagamore Hill (1908, brick-sized, never found).

## 5. Grades to reconsider

- **Heat markers graded medium or higher, against `credibility_test.md`:** Malden 1913 (white hot, fumes, eyebrows singed, buried 10 ft: textbook tall tale), Fairfield 1931 ("still hot" the next day), Clintonville 1903 ("too hot to hold"), Lewistown 1931, Hardin 1951.
- **Galesburg 1931 (medium):** the only source is a satirical Daily Worker column. Weak, unless the Smithsonian has the record.
- **Destination ≠ support.** Cañon City (Philadelphia → Smithsonian), Clintonville (Smithsonian), Morgan (Univ. of Utah), Saline Co. (Smithsonian) and Deep Gap (Nininger) all reached an active collector or expert of their day, yet none is catalogued. That is mild evidence the expert rejected the stone. Deep Gap especially: Nininger named and published nearly everything he accepted. Ask the Nininger correspondence at ASU's Center for Meteorite Studies before calling it strong.
- **Saline Co., Mo. 1917:** "mouth of the Black River, in this county" (Marshall dateline). The Black River doesn't run through Saline Co. This is almost certainly the Blackwater River, which matters for any land or record search.

## 6. Method gaps

1. **The read queue only sees destinations.** Ceredo, Cambridge, Sycamore, Woodburn, Grinnell and Danbury all scored 0-1. 706 unread fall reports (440 distinct) sit inside a fireball event seen from 2+ cities; 191 inside 3+. A second queue, "fall report inside a multi-city fireball", would have caught most of section 2. The 1898-06-03 and 1918-01-22 events were even hand-vetted, but the fireball vetting and the fall read never met.
2. **Dates parsed from "Tuesday" split events.** Grinnell got a 14-day window and its own event.
3. **Read speed.** The logged batches are 0.3-0.7 s per story (439 in 157 s, 121 in 85 s). That is a skim of excerpts, so misses like these are expected. A slower second read of the strong and medium tier would be worth it.
4. **Never followed up:**
   - The pilot leads marked "not yet searched": Benton Co. 1872, Fourche Mtn 1879, Huntington Valley 1879, Santa Clara 1870, Belmont/Greensburg 1914, Mississippi 1893, Illinois 1875.
   - The ranker's own trail list: Cedar Twp./Iowa Wesleyan 1870, Step Rock road 1880, Indianapolis police office 1872, Rev. Dr. Wood 1877.
   - South Bend 1876's backward search.
5. **Outreach gaps:** named trails with no email yet are Washington Island 1914 (Door County HS; buyer Charles Jensen), Phipsburg 1850 (Maine State Museum) and Franklinton 1887. Danbury goes to the Yale Peabody.
6. **Stale docs:** `results/falls/README.md` still says the 447 single-paper families are unread.
