I'm a computer science student researching 19th-century meteorite reports in digitized American newspapers. I came

The *Daily Kennebec Journal* (Augusta, Me.) of November 20, 1873, page 3, reports that **Capt. Ira C. Doe of Saco

who lived about two and a half miles out on the Portland road, saw a meteor approach his property. He heard a
whizzing sound and an explosion, and the next morning found scorched grass and broken earth in the meadow behind his
house, where he dug up the stone. It is described as weighing about fifteen pounds, dull lead-colored, oddly shaped,
with a pebbled or shrivelled surface. Scan: https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82014248/1873-11-20/ed-1/?sp=3
