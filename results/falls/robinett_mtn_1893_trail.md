# Robinett Mountain (near Dayton, Wash.), June 1893 (Simon Eger): trail notes

**Original report:** *Columbia Chronicle* (Dayton) 1893-06-24, p. 3, "Found a Meteor". Crop:
`img/dayton_chronicle_1893-06-24_eger.jpg`.
- "About two weeks ago" (so ~June 10, 1893) Simon **Eger** (not Eyer: the Washington Standard 1893-06-30 reprint
  misspelled it), who lived on Robinett Mountain, saw a meteor fall, located the spot, and next morning found the stone
  "imbedded in the earth", "about the size of a man's head".
- New detail not in the reprint: "Mr. Eger brought the specimen to August Engel, who has presented it to the
  CHRONICLE office where it can be seen by anyone."
- August Engel = Dayton merchant (ads and county bills all through 1893).

**Source:** CCRLD's Dayton Chronicle scans (Google Drive folder linked from ccrld.org/newspaper-archive/, 1878-1927,
one PDF per weekly issue, image-only). Ellen Brigham (director@ccrld.org) pointed us there 2026-11-17. PDFs download
with plain curl via drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=<file id>; file ids come from the folder page in Chrome.
OCR: Apple Vision on ~1100x1500 tiles (full pages get downscaled and lose most text).

**Read so far:** every issue 1893-06-10 to 1893-12-16 except 08-12 (download failed). Searched for meteor, Eger/Eyer,
Engel, aerolite, Robinett. Nothing more on the stone. Unrelated: 1893-10-07 p3, a brilliant meteor with a roaring
noise passed north "Monday evening" (Oct 2); no landing claimed.

**1894-1895 read too (2026-11-17), plus the missing 1893-08-12:** all 105 issues OCR'd (15,210 tiles) and searched
for meteor, aerolite, Eger/Egger, Engel, Robinett and "Chronicle office". Nothing more on the stone. What turned up:
- The man is spelled **Simon Egger** in later items: "Simon Egger, the well-known pioneer trapper and hunter" (1894-01-06
  p1, a mountain correspondence column, trapping marten on the head of Little Salmon); "Simon Egger is hunting and
  trapping" (1895-11-30 p2); back from a hunt (1895-12-14 p2). So he was still around Dayton's mountain country in 1895.
- 1894-02-24 p2 "falling meteor ... at the foot of a steep hill" on Johnson Hollow is a joke about a man falling down a
  hill (boots, red head), not a meteor.
- 1895-08-17 p4: reprint from the Gold Beach (Ore.) Gazette, a bright meteor/aerolite that burst near Doyle's on the
  Rogue River; fragments fell. Unrelated to Dayton, but a possible new lead for the sweep.
